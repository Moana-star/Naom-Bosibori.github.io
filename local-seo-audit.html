Independent sample · Local SEO · Audit method

From local search signals to a practical action plan.

This demonstration shows how I would structure a first-pass local SEO review for a home-service business: clarify what customers need, separate observed evidence from assumptions, prioritize checks, and turn findings into actions a business owner can understand.

Scope and transparency: This is a hypothetical example for a fictional home-service business—not an audit of a real company, a verified Google Business Profile review, or a claim of measured results. The issues below are audit questions and illustrative scenarios, not findings from a live website. A real assessment would require the actual business details, website, profile URL and available performance data.

01 / The brief

Start with the customer and the business goal.

Scenario: “Northstar Home Repairs” is a fictional local service provider that wants nearby customers to find its services, understand its coverage area, and contact the business with less friction.

Primary objective

Qualified local enquiries

Help people in the service area understand what the business offers and take the next step by calling or submitting a useful enquiry.

Review boundaries

Discovery, relevance and conversion

Check the public-facing information and website path first. Do not promise rankings or infer performance without suitable evidence.

Questions before starting: Which services matter most? Which locations does the business genuinely serve? Does it visit customers at their locations or receive them at a staffed address? Which enquiries count as qualified? Who can approve profile and website changes?

03 / Findings format

Make every recommendation traceable.

A useful audit does not simply list SEO terminology. It explains what was checked, what the evidence shows, what is still uncertain, and what the business can do next.

Illustrative scenario · High priority

Contact details may conflict

Evidence needed: capture the actual phone and business details from the profile, website and selected listings. Recommendation: verify the correct details with the business owner, then update incorrect high-visibility references. Validation: recheck the corrected pages and test the phone link.

Illustrative scenario · High priority

The main service page may not answer key questions

Evidence needed: review the page for service scope, coverage, process, limitations and a clear contact route. Recommendation: fill genuine information gaps and add relevant internal links. Validation: confirm the page accurately reflects the business and test the contact path.

Illustrative scenario · Medium priority

Profile services may be incomplete

Evidence needed: compare the eligible profile fields with the services the business actually provides. Recommendation: complete relevant categories and service details without keyword stuffing. Validation: review the published profile for accuracy after edits.

Illustrative scenario · Needs diagnosis

Important pages may be difficult to discover

Evidence needed: inspect internal links, sitemap entries, response status and indexability signals for selected URLs. Recommendation: fix a confirmed access or discovery issue rather than guessing. Validation: repeat the same checks after implementation.

Important: These examples illustrate the reporting format only. No issue should be reported as a confirmed finding until the corresponding evidence has actually been collected.

04 / Prioritization

Sequence work by impact, confidence and effort.

Confirm the basics

Verify profile eligibility and accuracy, business contact details, website availability and the main enquiry path. These checks help prevent obvious customer-facing problems.

Resolve verified technical barriers

Prioritize confirmed problems affecting important pages, such as a broken URL, unintended redirect, blocking directive or non-working contact form. Document the exact evidence.

Improve service-page usefulness

Make priority services and genuine coverage clear, answer likely customer questions, and provide meaningful links and calls to action.

Measure and refine

Set a baseline and agree on meaningful measures before changes. Review relevant Search Console data, profile performance where available, website visits and qualified enquiries over a suitable period.

05 / Measurement and limits

Measure what the business can actually observe.

  • Search visibility: relevant impressions, clicks and queries in Search Console, where available.
  • Profile interactions: available performance indicators in the business profile, interpreted in context.
  • Website actions: successful calls, form submissions or other agreed conversion events—not just page views.
  • Lead quality: whether enquiries come from the right service area and match the services offered.
  • Implementation checks: whether the agreed fixes were completed and the original issue can no longer be reproduced.

Local rankings vary with the searcher, location, competition and other factors. I would not guarantee a particular ranking, lead volume or conversion increase. I would report the work completed, evidence reviewed, limitations and observed changes without attributing outcomes to a single change unless the data supports that conclusion.

06 / References

Primary guidance I would consult.

  • Google Business Profile Help: Guidelines for representing your business on Google — business eligibility and accurate representation.
  • Google Business Profile Help: Improve your local ranking on Google — Google's published guidance on relevance, distance and prominence.
  • Google Search Central: SEO Starter Guide — foundational website and search guidance.
  • Google Search Central: Introduction to robots.txt — understanding crawl rules and their limits.
  • Google Search Central: Get started with Search Console — monitoring and investigating search performance.
How I would apply this to a real client: confirm the scope and access first, collect evidence from the actual website and eligible business profile, document verified findings, agree on priorities, and deliver a concise action plan with clear limitations.
