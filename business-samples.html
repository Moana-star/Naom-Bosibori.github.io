Sample 11 · Developer documentation · SaaS onboarding

Illustrative scenario

Connect your first workspace: a practical SaaS API onboarding guide

Business goal: Help a new technical customer complete a first successful integration with fewer setup errors and a clear route to troubleshooting.

Before you begin

You need an active workspace, an API key with the minimum required permissions, and access to a test environment. Create a separate key for each environment. Never place a production secret in browser code, a public repository or a support ticket.

1. Create and protect an API key

  1. Open your workspace's developer settings.
  2. Create a key for the integration you are configuring.
  3. Grant only the scopes required for the first workflow.
  4. Store the key in your server-side environment configuration.

2. Send a test request

Use the documented endpoint and your test key to request the current workspace profile. Confirm that the response belongs to the expected workspace before connecting additional systems.

GET /v1/workspace
Authorization: Bearer YOUR_TEST_KEY
Accept: application/json

3. Check the result

A successful response should include the workspace identifier and the fields documented for your account. If the request fails, check the HTTP status, key environment, permissions and request format before rotating credentials.

When setup does not work

  • 401: confirm the key is present, current and sent using the documented authentication scheme.
  • 403: check whether the key has the required scope and whether the account permits the operation.
  • 429: respect the service's rate-limit guidance and retry policy.

Completion check: the test request returns the expected workspace, the key remains server-side, and the integration owner records which environment was configured.

Sample 12 · Product documentation · Ecommerce payment support

Illustrative scenario

Order placed, payment pending: a customer-friendly troubleshooting guide

Business goal: Reduce confusion during checkout support by explaining what a pending payment means and what evidence support staff should check before responding.

A pending payment does not necessarily mean that an order has been paid or that the payment has failed. The checkout provider may still be processing the transaction, or the store may not yet have received a verified status update.

What customers should do

  1. Keep the order reference shown at checkout.
  2. Check the payment provider's transaction activity.
  3. Avoid submitting the same payment repeatedly while the first attempt is unresolved.
  4. Contact the store through its published support channel if the status remains unclear.

What support teams should verify

  • Match the order reference, amount and currency.
  • Check the provider's authoritative transaction status.
  • Confirm whether the provider reference belongs to this order.
  • Record the time checked and the evidence used.

Suggested support response

“Your order is recorded, but we have not yet confirmed the payment. Please do not pay again just yet. We are checking the transaction with our payment provider and will update you once its status is verified.”

Important: A screenshot, browser redirect or customer message can help locate a transaction, but it should not replace the payment provider's verified status when deciding whether an order is paid.

Sample 13 · SEO content · Local service business

Illustrative scenario

Local service page brief: make the service, location and next step clear

Business goal: Help a local service business create a useful landing page that answers customer questions and makes contacting the business straightforward.

Recommended page structure

  1. Specific headline: name the service and the verified service area.
  2. Service explanation: describe what is included, who it is for and what customers should prepare.
  3. Coverage: list only locations the business genuinely serves.
  4. Process: explain enquiry, assessment, quotation and delivery steps.
  5. Evidence: use genuine staff credentials, original photos, real reviews with permission and accurate business details.
  6. Contact: provide a working phone, email or enquiry route with a clear response expectation.

Example page introduction

“Need help with [service] in [verified service area]? We explain the available options, what the work involves and how to request an assessment. Share a few details about your requirements and our team will confirm whether the service is a good fit.”

SEO and quality checks

  • Use one clear page title and a descriptive meta description.
  • Keep the service and location consistent across the page and genuine business listings.
  • Add useful internal links to pricing, FAQs and relevant service details.
  • Do not create near-duplicate pages for locations the business does not serve.
  • Verify all claims, opening hours, addresses and contact details before publication.

Success measure to define with the business: qualified enquiries from the intended service area—not rankings or traffic alone.

Sample 14 · AI product content · Customer support knowledge base

Illustrative scenario

Prepare your help centre for an AI support assistant

Business goal: Make support information easier to retrieve while reducing the risk that an AI assistant gives outdated or unsupported answers.

An AI support assistant is only as dependable as the information and controls behind it. Before connecting a help centre, organise the source material so that each answer can be traced to a current, approved article.

Content preparation checklist

  • Assign an owner and review date to every policy and help article.
  • Separate current instructions from archived or superseded guidance.
  • Use clear titles that describe the customer's task or question.
  • Break long procedures into numbered steps with prerequisites and expected outcomes.
  • Record product version, region or plan restrictions where they change the answer.

Assistant response rules

  1. Answer from approved help-centre sources.
  2. Link to the source article and identify when information may vary by plan or region.
  3. Ask a clarifying question when the request lacks essential details.
  4. Say when the source material does not answer the question.
  5. Escalate account-specific, financial or high-impact requests to an authorised support route.

Evaluation before release

Build a test set from real support intents only when those examples are appropriately authorised and privacy-reviewed. Include common questions, ambiguous requests, outdated instructions, conflicting articles and questions with no approved answer. Review answer correctness, source relevance, escalation behaviour and unsupported claims.

Release principle: do not measure success only by how often the assistant responds. Measure whether it gives supported answers, cites the right material and escalates when it should.

Sample 15 · Workflow documentation · AI-assisted lead qualification

Illustrative scenario

Document an AI-assisted lead qualification workflow without overstating automation

Business goal: Help a sales team understand which tasks are automated, which information is used and where a person must review the recommendation.

Workflow at a glance

  1. Capture: receive an enquiry through an approved form or CRM integration.
  2. Validate: check required fields, consent status and duplicate records.
  3. Enrich: retrieve only approved business information from authorised sources.
  4. Summarise: generate a draft summary with source references and missing information clearly marked.
  5. Review: a sales representative checks fit, accuracy and next steps.
  6. Record: save the approved outcome and the reviewer identity in the CRM.

What the AI may and may not do

  • May: organise submitted information, identify missing fields and suggest a draft category based on documented criteria.
  • Must not: invent company details, infer sensitive characteristics, silently reject a person or change CRM records without the required permission.

Example review record

Recommendation: Needs more information.

Reason: The enquiry describes the requested service but does not specify organisation size or implementation timing.

Next action: Ask the prospect for the missing details. No automatic qualification decision is recorded.

Operational checks

Document access permissions, data retention, model and prompt versions, failure handling, human override, duplicate prevention and audit fields. Test incomplete, misleading and conflicting enquiries before enabling the workflow for a live team.

Sample 16 · Product communication · SaaS release notes

Illustrative scenario

Release notes customers can act on: a clear update for a dashboard change

Business goal: Explain a product change in a way that helps customers understand what changed, who is affected and whether they need to do anything.

Dashboard navigation update

What changed: The workspace navigation has been reorganised so that reporting and account settings are grouped separately from daily activity.

Why it changed: The revised structure is intended to make frequently used workspace areas easier to locate. This is a usability update; it does not change account permissions or report data.

Who is affected: Users of the updated workspace interface.

What you need to do: No migration is required. Your existing reports and saved workspace information remain available in their current locations.

Before publishing release notes

  • Confirm the feature is actually deployed and identify the affected versions or plans.
  • Verify every statement about data, permissions and migration with the product team.
  • Explain changed navigation or workflows with updated screenshots or links where available.
  • State known limitations and support routes.
  • Remove claims about performance improvements unless supported by measured evidence.

Editorial principle: a useful release note answers four questions quickly: what changed, why it matters, who is affected and what the reader needs to do.
